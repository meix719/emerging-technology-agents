Potential provider savings are distinct from hospital-captured value. These causal differences support different provisional exposure judgments and management priorities without forcing different outcomes.

## Remaining limitations

- Hypothetical business inputs: organizational capabilities, revenue models, coverage constraints, and potential advantages are supplied assumptions, not verified provider facts.
- No validated local economics: volumes, margins, exposed contribution, payer/contract terms, vendor/integration/support costs, demand, and value capture are not established.
- No validated local performance: no named product/version has been tested for the local population, task, quality, correction burden, signed-report performance, or service continuity. Exact jurisdiction and operational suitability remain unresolved.
- Unsupported forecast timing: no sufficient dated market, adoption, competitor, referral, or contract evidence establishes a material-impact arrival category. Conditional scenarios must remain labeled as such.
- Limited testing: two contexts, one initial response and one conversational rerun per context, one instruction revision, and no independent or blinded evaluation. The narrow assisted task does not establish whole-industry or enterprise exposure.
- One conversational rerun does not establish repeatability. Conversational carryover, generation variation, and shared evidence may influence results; these observations do not isolate a causal effect of the instruction revision.
- Structural validation checks the supplied output contract; it does not verify sources, forecast validity, clinical safety, economic value, or analytical quality. Original research results do not automatically generalize to these hypothetical organizations.

## Independent professional judgment

I see AI-assisted imaging interpretation mainly as an opportunity to support radiologists. For the hypothetical independent radiology practice, it could improve reporting accuracy and efficiency, but these benefits must be demonstrated locally. Continued human review does not guarantee that staffing, responsibilities, or interpretation fees will remain unchanged.

Management should introduce the technology gradually, beginning with a limited, supervised evaluation of accuracy, feasibility, total review and correction effort, and full costs. Expansion should depend on demonstrated value while preserving qualified radiologist review and final sign-off.

My escalation trigger would be insurers reducing reimbursement for interpretation because they expect AI-assisted work to cost less. This is a hypothetical development to monitor, not an observed payer policy. Management should determine whether a change would directly affect payments billed by the practice or reach it through renegotiated client contracts, considering the relevant billing arrangements and contract terms.

The timing of material business-model effects remains uncertain. A Moderate-severity/Mid-term placement could serve as a conditional planning scenario if validated adoption leads to reimbursement or contract pressure during that period; it is not a supported forecast. Under that scenario, monitoring and building optionality would be appropriate, with stronger action depending on documented financial exposure.
