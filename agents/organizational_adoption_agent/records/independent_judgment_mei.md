**Technology:** Mako-assisted elective total knee replacement  
**Case:** Hypothetical NHS teaching Hospital A  
**Decision:** Delay clinical adoption, not permanently reject it.

## Student's professional judgment

I would delay Hospital A's clinical adoption of Mako-assisted knee replacement—not reject the technology—until the hospital verifies patient safety and long-term financial sustainability. My first priority is patient safety: documented training for participating surgeons and theatre teams, supervised procedures or proctoring, tested emergency backup and conversion plans, and credible evidence from comparable hospitals. The hospital also needs clinical governance approval, reliable CT/planning workflows, vendor support, and a funded operating plan. Readiness alone does not make adoption worthwhile. I would want stronger comparative evidence of faster recovery, fewer repeat surgeries, and better long-term patient outcomes. Once both clinical value and all safety, financial, and operational conditions are demonstrated, I would support a limited, monitored pilot with trained surgeons and teams—not immediate hospital-wide rollout. If a six-month pilot showed faster recovery but higher operating costs, I would pause expansion and reassess business value. I would examine whether shorter hospital stays, fewer readmissions, and improved hospital efficiency offset the additional costs. Missing evidence on clinical value, safety approvals, or sustainable finances could change my decision.
