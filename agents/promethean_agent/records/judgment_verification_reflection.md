# Independent Professional Judgment

I classify autonomous driving systems used for driverless taxis as a breakthrough within demonstrated operating conditions, with moderate confidence. They change how transportation services operate by removing the need for a human driver in some situations, but the evidence reviewed does not establish civilization-wide dependence on them. Existing Waymo operations demonstrate that the management question concerns introducing or expanding an existing capability rather than proving the idea for the first time.

For the hypothetical urban company, I recommend gradual introduction in a selected area while keeping human-driven taxis available. Expansion should depend on demonstrated safety, reliability, accessibility, passenger acceptance, and sustainable costs. Broader technological significance should encourage investigation, but should not determine adoption by itself.

I would reconsider a potentially Promethean classification if essential transportation systems became durably dependent on autonomy and human-driven alternatives could no longer practically replace their functions. Cybercab illustrates a vehicle designed around autonomy; it does not by itself demonstrate societal dependence. Public trust may affect adoption, but discomfort alone does not determine the significance classification.
